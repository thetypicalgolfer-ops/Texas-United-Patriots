HB 236 Asks Texas to Declare February 4 Muslim Heritage Day. Lawmakers Should Say No.

November 10, 2026 | 2 min read | Texas United Patriots Staff
Spread the Word

Among the first bills filed for the 2027 session is House Bill 236, by Rep. Suleman Lalani, the same lawmaker who filed HB 613's universal background checks. It would make February 4 "Muslim Heritage Day" in Texas.

What the Bill Says

HB 236 designates February 4 as Muslim Heritage Day "in recognition of the historic, cultural, and social contributions of Muslim Americans," to be observed with ceremonies and activities. It is a day of observance, not a paid state holiday, and it would take effect September 1, 2027.

This Is Not About Our Neighbors

Muslim Texans are our neighbors, our coworkers and our fellow citizens, and they are as free as every other Texan to celebrate their faith and heritage. Nobody needs the Legislature's permission to do that.

It Is About What Government Is For

The question is whether the State of Texas should be in the business of passing laws that honor one religion's heritage. Once it starts, every faith and every group will rightly ask for its own day, and the Legislature becomes a referee of whose heritage gets official recognition. The better rule is the simple one: government protects every Texan's freedom of religion and does not sponsor any of them.

Priorities

The Legislature meets for 140 days. In that time it has to deal with property taxes, the border, school choice, the grid and a wave of gun-control bills. A ceremonial religious day is not why Texans sent anyone to Austin.

What You Can Do

Tell your state representative you want the 2027 session spent on Texans' real problems, and that you oppose HB 236.

Quick Answers

What is Texas HB 236?

HB 236, filed November 9, 2026 by Rep. Suleman Lalani, would designate February 4 as Muslim Heritage Day in Texas.

Is Muslim Heritage Day under HB 236 a state holiday?

No. HB 236 creates a day of observance that may be marked with ceremonies and activities; it is not a paid state holiday. It would take effect September 1, 2027.
