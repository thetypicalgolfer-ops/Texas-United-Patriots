10 Red Flags Your Child's School Renamed CRT Instead of Removing It

July 25, 2026 | 7 min read | Texas United Patriots Staff
Spread the Word

Texas parents were told the fight was over in 2021. Ask a teacher what changed in the actual curriculum and you will usually get a careful answer. Here are ten things you can check yourself — no lawyer, no lawsuit, no insider access required.

1. The Word Changed. The Framework Didn't.

Look for "equity," "culturally responsive teaching," "social-emotional learning," or "identity-safe classrooms." None of these are automatically a problem. All of them are common relabels. Ask what the curriculum actually teaches, not what the folder is called.

2. You're Told the Materials Are Unavailable

A district that cannot produce the lesson plan a child was taught yesterday is telling you something. Curriculum is a public record in Texas. "Proprietary vendor content" is not a legal exemption from a parent's question.

3. Training Is Mandatory for Staff, Optional to Disclose to You

Teacher professional development is where the framework usually enters a district. If staff sat through a training you are not permitted to review, that is the document to request.

4. The Vendor Is National, the Oversight Is Nobody

Ask who approved the third-party provider, at which board meeting, and by what vote. If no trustee can answer, the district outsourced its curriculum judgment.

5. Children Are Sorted by Group Before They're Taught Anything

Exercises that assign students a position based on race, sex, or "privilege" are the signature. Watch for classroom activities framed as "positionality" or "identity mapping."

6. Surveys Ask Your Child About Home

Climate surveys and SEL screeners frequently collect information on family beliefs, income, and household composition — often without meaningful consent. Ask what is collected, who stores it, and how long it is kept.

7. The Board Answers Questions That Weren't Asked

A trustee who responds to a specific curriculum question with a general statement about inclusion is deflecting. Repeat the original question, in writing, at the next meeting.

8. Public Comment Was Quietly Restructured

Shorter speaking limits, sign-ups closed earlier, comment moved after the vote. Procedure is where parental input goes to die, and it rarely makes the news.

9. Opting Out Is Technically Allowed and Practically Impossible

If the opt-out requires a form nobody can locate, from a staffer who is unavailable, before a deadline you were never told, the right exists on paper only.

10. Everyone Insists It Isn't Happening — Nobody Will Show You

The single most reliable indicator. Districts with nothing to hide hand over the curriculum. If your question keeps producing reassurance instead of documents, the reassurance is the answer.

What To Do With This List

Print it. Take it to the next board meeting. Go through it item by item and ask for documents, not assurances — the lesson plan, the vendor contract, the training deck, the survey instrument. Put every request in writing and keep the date.

A district operating in good faith will find this easy. That is the whole point of the exercise: it costs an honest district nothing and an evasive one everything.

Check Your Own District

Work through the ten flags at your next board meeting and send us what you find. We publish what parents document.
